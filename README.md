⚙️ Activity 4 — Automation vs Artificial Intelligence
One important learning from Day 2 was:

Automation ≠ Artificial Intelligence

Not every automated aviation process uses AI.

Example
An airline may have a predefined rule:

Flight status changes

↓

Business rule

↓

SMS automatically sent

This is software automation because the system follows a predefined rule. It is not necessarily AI.

Comparison
Technology	What it Does	Aviation Example
Traditional AI	Predicts, detects, classifies, optimises or recommends	Predicting flight delays
Generative AI	Creates new content	Writing a passenger delay message
Both	AI analysis followed by content generation	Predicting a delay + generating a notification
Automation	Follows predefined rules	Automatically sending an SMS
Human Judgement	Takes responsibility for high-stakes decisions	Final aircraft airworthiness decision
Key Learning
An aviation organisation should first identify the business problem and then decide whether the solution should involve:

Automation
Traditional AI
Generative AI
A combination of AI
Authorised human judgement

⚠️ Activity 10 — Hallucination Challenge

The lab examined a passenger communication concerning a cancelled flight.

The important principle was:

Fluency ≠ Accuracy

An AI response can sound professional and confident while still containing unsupported information.

Information Requiring Verification

Before communicating passenger entitlements, verify:

Compensation amount or eligibility
Hotel/accommodation entitlement
Meal or refreshment entitlement
Refund eligibility and timeline
Applicable passenger-rights regulations
Ticket conditions
Official Sources

The information should be checked against:

The airline's official policy
Ticket conditions
Applicable aviation regulator
Government authority
🔎 Improved Prompt

A safer prompt for aviation passenger communication is:

Rewrite the passenger response without inventing any airline policy, compensation amount, passenger entitlement, regulation, hotel or meal entitlement, or refund timeline. Wherever verified information is required, write [VERIFY OFFICIAL POLICY/REGULATION]. Clearly distinguish between information that is known and information that still requires verification. Do not assume the airline, country, route, ticket conditions, or applicable regulations.

🛡️ Responsible AI in Aviation

The lab demonstrates several important responsible-AI principles:

1. Human Oversight

Humans should review important AI outputs.

2. Verification

AI-generated information should not automatically be treated as verified.

3. Privacy

Customer information should only be used appropriately and with relevant consent.

4. Accountability

AI should support employees rather than remove human responsibility.

5. Safety

Safety-critical aviation decisions should remain under authorised human control.

💡 Final Reflection
1. What is the main difference between Traditional AI and Generative AI?

Traditional AI mainly predicts, detects, classifies, optimises or recommends using data, while Generative AI creates new content such as text, images, summaries or messages.

2. Where can both work together?

An airline can use Traditional AI to predict a flight delay and Generative AI to draft a personalised notification.

3. What is one limitation of ChatGPT?

ChatGPT may produce a professional-sounding response without verifying the airline's actual policy or applicable regulation.

4. What is an aviation hallucination?

An example would be an AI inventing a specific compensation amount or refund timeline without being provided with the relevant airline policy or regulation.

5. What requires human judgement?

Human verification and final approval are required before sending passenger communications or taking consequential business actions.

6. Why is human verification important?

Aviation involves safety, regulatory, legal and operational consequences. Incorrect information can affect passengers and aviation operations.

7. How can AI improve aviation?

AI can help airlines:

Identify passenger needs faster
Predict disruptions
Create personalised communication
Reduce routine workload
Support employees in operational and business processes
8. Which decisions should not rely solely on Generative AI?

Generative AI should not solely make decisions involving:

Aircraft airworthiness
Emergency actions
Safety investigations
Regulatory compliance
Significant safety responsibilities
Significant legal responsibilities
📚 Key Takeaways
Traditional AI predicts, detects, classifies and recommends.
Generative AI creates new content.
Both can work together in aviation workflows.
Automation is not necessarily AI.
Human judgement remains important for high-stakes aviation decisions.
Customer data requires privacy and consent considerations.
AI-generated content should be reviewed before being used.
Professional language does not guarantee factual accuracy.
Aviation policies and passenger entitlements should be verified from authoritative sources.
AI should support aviation professionals rather than replace accountability.
